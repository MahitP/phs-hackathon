Every school day, someone decides whether to open the windows.

Most days it's a guess. SkyWindow answers with data, and the data starts 824 km up.

This is the Earth right now, lit by the real sun.

Satellites watch for fire.

NASA's Suomi NPP passes over twice a day. Its VIIRS camera spots fires by their heat, and SkyWindow downloads those detections every hour to see whether smoke could reach PHS.

Air is a column.

Smoke, dust and haze fill it from the ground to the edge of space. Scientists measure how much sunlight that column blocks and call it aerosol optical depth.

The sunbeam test.

The thicker the haze and the lower the sun, the less of the direct beam reaches the ground. It's the Beer–Lambert law, the same physics that turns sunsets red.

T = e−AOD / sin θ

On a classroom windowsill sits SkyWindow.

A Raspberry Pi, two sensors and a small screen. Once a second it compares the sky outside with the air in the room.

Five parts, off the shelf.

Grove sensors plug into a Raspberry Pi with Grove cables. No soldering.

The air sensor

A Grove gas sensor that reacts to cleaning sprays, markers and stale air. SkyWindow turns it into a stale index from 0 to 100, measured against the room's own clean baseline. It never pretends to be an AQI.

The light sensor

It faces the sky. Calibrated against clear-sky sunlight for this time and place, it shows how much light really gets through.

The Raspberry Pi

It reads both sensors, pulls the outdoor data, checks NASA fire detections upwind and decides once a second. A model we trained on our own labelled readings recognises what's happening in the room.

The screen and the speaker

One answer in one color, readable from across the room, and read out loud when it changes.

October 6, noon. AQI 23 outside, cleaner than the room, so the sash goes up.

Tonight. AQI 60 outside and a fresh room, so it barely matters.

June 7, 2023. Wildfire smoke, AQI 147. Keep it shut and run a fan instead.

Someone sprays cleaner and the room spikes. Open up until it clears.

Then it says it out loud. Gemini writes one sentence from the facts, and an ElevenLabs voice reads it to the class:

“SkyWindow update: the air in this room just spiked, so ventilate right away.”
